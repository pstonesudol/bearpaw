Audio & Transcription

Bearpaw can record the audio of each scanner hit and transcribe it on your Mac. Everything stays on the computer: audio is written to a folder you choose, and transcription runs locally with whisper.cpp. Nothing is uploaded.

What you need

  • An audio input on your Mac. A USB-C capture adapter with a 3.5 mm line input works well; the Cubilux adapter is what this was designed against.
  • A 3.5 mm cable from the scanner's headphone jack to the adapter's line input. The BC125AT has no speaker output.
  • The scanner's USB cable, as usual. Control and audio are separate connections; unplugging one does not stop the other.

The scanner has one receiver, so Bearpaw records one transmission at a time. That is a property of the radio, not a limitation of the app.

Turn it on

  1. Open Device → Preferences → Audio & Transcription and choose Configure.
  2. Turn on Record scanner hits, then pick your adapter under Input device. The level meter should move when the scanner receives a signal. If it does not, check the macOS microphone permission below.
  3. Use Test recording to capture a few seconds and confirm the adapter is delivering audio.

Recording is off until you turn it on. While it is on, Bearpaw keeps a short rolling buffer so the start of a transmission is not clipped, and keeps recording briefly after the squelch closes so the end is not cut off.

macOS microphone permission

macOS treats any audio input as a microphone. The first time Bearpaw records, it asks for permission. If you dismissed the prompt, enable Bearpaw under System Settings → Privacy & Security → Microphone and relaunch.

A denied permission can produce a file of silence rather than an error. If recordings are silent, check this first. If you run the backend from a terminal instead of the app, the terminal is the app that needs permission.

Transcription

Transcription needs two things Bearpaw does not bundle: the whisper-cli program and a model file. Install them once: 

brew install whisper-cpp
mkdir -p "$HOME/Library/Application Support/Bearpaw/models"
curl -L -o "$HOME/Library/Application Support/Bearpaw/models/ggml-base.en.bin" \
  https://huggingface.co/ggerganov/whisper.cpp/resolve/main/ggml-base.en.bin

base.en is a good default: about 150 MB and faster than real time on Apple Silicon. Models in the Bearpaw models folder appear in the selector automatically. Both the program and the model can be pointed at explicitly in the same settings sheet.

Vocabulary is a comma-separated list of words whisper should spell correctly — your railroads, interlockings, and local place names. A short list of words that actually come up helps more than a long glossary. Bearpaw ships a starting list for SEPTA, Norfolk Southern and Amtrak traffic.

What you get

  • A small microphone marker on a Recent Hit means its audio was kept. A dot beside it shows transcription state: green finished, amber working, red failed.
  • Select a hit to open its details: a player, the full transcript, and buttons to correct the transcript, transcribe again, or delete the recording.
  • History next to Recent Hits searches every stored hit by channel name, frequency, date range, transcript text, whether audio exists, and transcription state.
  • Correcting a transcript never destroys the machine version. Bearpaw keeps it and shows it under Machine transcript.

Storage and retention

Audio is written to recordings in Bearpaw's application data folder unless you choose another folder in the settings sheet. Files are 16 kHz mono WAV, about 32 KB per second of audio.

Keep audio for controls how long files stay. Setting it to 0 keeps audio until the hit itself ages out under Data Retention. When audio expires or is deleted, the hit and its timestamps stay in history; only the recording and transcript go.

Limits worth knowing

  • Hit boundaries come from the scanner's squelch, which Bearpaw polls five times a second. A recording can start up to about 200 ms after the transmission actually began. The pre-roll is there to cover that gap, not to eliminate it.
  • If the scanner changes frequency without the squelch closing, the file cannot be split into two transmissions. Bearpaw keeps the audio and labels it partial rather than attributing it to one frequency.
  • Unplugging the audio adapter mid-transmission keeps the audio written so far and marks it partial. Scanning continues.
  • Transcripts are best-effort. Radio noise, clipped audio and local proper nouns all cause errors. Listen to the recording before relying on a transcript.

Troubleshooting

Symptom Check
No microphone marker on hits Recording is on, and the hit is longer than Minimum recording.
Files are silent macOS microphone permission for Bearpaw (or your terminal).
“Audio input device not found” The adapter is unplugged or renamed. Re-select it; capture retries every few seconds.
“whisper-cli was not found” Install it with brew install whisper-cpp or set its path.
“No whisper model found” Put a GGML .bin model in the Bearpaw models folder, or select one.
Transcript is nonsense Add the local names to Vocabulary, and check the audio is not clipped.
